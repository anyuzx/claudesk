Abstract

Chromatin relaxation in living nuclei reflects the combined effects of polymer connectivity, transient molecular contacts, active remodeling, and heterogeneous nuclear microenvironments. While equilibrium polymer models can reproduce many static structural observables, they often fail to capture the broad spectrum of stress-relaxation timescales observed in live-cell measurements and particle-based simulations. Here we introduce a generalized Langevin framework for chromatin stress relaxation that represents unresolved molecular degrees of freedom through a compact set of memory modes coupled to coarse-grained polymer coordinates.

Using simulated contact maps, single-locus tracking statistics, and stress autocorrelation functions, we show that the proposed model recovers both short-time viscoelastic response and long-time relaxation tails across a range of chromatin compaction states. The inferred memory spectrum separates fast local contact fluctuations from slower collective rearrangements and provides interpretable parameters for comparing cell states, perturbations, and model resolutions. These results suggest a practical route for extending distance-map-based chromatin ensemble models toward predictive nonequilibrium mechanics.
