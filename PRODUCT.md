# Product

## Register

product

## Users

claudesk is for individual researchers who need a private, durable workspace for reading, organizing, and acting on scientific literature. The primary users include master's students, Ph.D. students, postdocs, principal investigators, professors, independent researchers, industry scientists, and technically fluent research staff.

Users are usually working alone inside an ongoing research context: scanning new papers, deciding what matters, connecting papers to projects, tracking tasks, recording progress, and asking an assistant to reason over their own local research memory. They may be domain experts, early-career researchers learning a field, or independent scientists building their own literature workflow.

## Product Purpose

claudesk helps a researcher turn scattered literature, notes, tasks, and project context into a coherent local research workspace. It fetches and ranks papers, stores personal reading state and notes, links papers to projects, tracks tasks and progress, and provides a chat agent that can use the local research database plus selected external tools.

Success means the researcher can quickly answer: what is new, what is worth reading, what project does this belong to, what did I decide, and what should I do next. The product should shorten the path from paper discovery to research action without forcing a team workflow, cloud account, or generic productivity model.

## Brand Personality

Precise, quiet, research-native.

The product should feel like an instrument panel for research work: compact, legible, trusted, and calm under repeated daily use. It should respect expert users, avoid cheerleading, and let the researcher stay in flow. The assistant should feel like a capable research aide attached to the user's own archive, not a general chatbot pasted into a note app.

## Anti-references

claudesk should not look or behave like a SaaS marketing dashboard, social productivity suite, generic AI chat wrapper, student flashcard app, or decorative note-taking product. Avoid onboarding that treats researchers like beginners in their own domain. Avoid gamification, celebratory effects, oversized marketing sections, generic empty-state illustrations, and broad productivity slogans.

It should also avoid becoming too discipline-specific in its language. The default framing should work for biology, physics, chemistry, medicine, engineering, computational science, and interdisciplinary research unless a user configures narrower profile settings.

## Design Principles

1. Research memory first: every workflow should help the user preserve useful context and retrieve it later.
2. Time to evidence: new users should quickly see how papers, projects, tasks, log entries, and chat connect around real research material.
3. Expert-respecting defaults: teach the product model without over-explaining familiar research concepts.
4. Local trust: make private, local-first behavior feel clear and dependable.
5. Dense but calm: support serious repeated use through scanable layouts, stable controls, and restrained feedback.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface should remain usable in both dark and light themes, with strong text contrast, keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus states, and no essential information communicated by color alone. Motion should be minimal, state-driven, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.

Copy should be discipline-neutral by default and should not assume a particular career stage, institution type, or publication domain. The product should support users who are expert researchers but new to claudesk, as well as technically confident users who want to skip onboarding and work directly.
